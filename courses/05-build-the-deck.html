LiveWhy the build comes fifth, not first3 min▶

Most people start here. They open a tool, type a topic, and accept whatever comes out. That is why so many decks look related to each other and land with nobody.

By putting the build fifth, everything the tool generates has something to be judged against: a thesis it either supports or does not, headlines that either state a message or do not, figures that either trace to a source or come off the slide. The tool becomes a typist rather than an author, which is the only role it is actually good at.

Real world

The single most common way a deck gets rebuilt from scratch: someone generated forty slides on Monday, showed them Wednesday, and discovered the argument was wrong. The generation was the cheap part. The three days spent polishing the wrong argument were not.

Part 1 · covers licensing reality

The licence trap, taught first because it wastes the most time

5 min live

You will click a Copilot button, nothing useful will happen, and no message will tell you why. This is not a bug and it is documented. Knowing it in advance is worth more than any prompt technique in this course.

LiveThe Designer licence, in Microsoft's own words4 min▶

Microsoft's PowerPoint FAQ lists the features that require a separate Microsoft Designer licence: adding a slide, creating a presentation about a specific topic, creating a presentation from a file, organizing the presentation, and adding an image.

Without it you still get, again in Microsoft's wording, "summarization, open-ended Q and A prompting, and basic chat features". Which is to say: the half of Copilot that reads your deck works, and the half that writes it does not.

Which is session 2's advice, arrived at from the other direction.

Self-studyClaude for PowerPoint, and the requirement list that bites4 min▶

Generally available to Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise plans. It reads the slide master, layouts, fonts and colour scheme, makes pinpoint edits to a single slide without regenerating the deck, and produces native PowerPoint charts, described in the docs as "visuals you can edit directly, not static images". That last point matters: an editable chart survives a reviewer, a picture of a chart does not.

Where it will not run, and this list is specific: PowerPoint 2016 or 2019 perpetual and volume licence, PowerPoint on iPad, PowerPoint on Android, and Microsoft 365 builds older than 16.0.13127.20296 on Windows or 16.46 on Mac. The web version works.

Anthropic's own limitations list names three things it is not recommended for: final client deliverables without human review, presentations containing highly sensitive or regulated data without proper controls, and replacing your judgment on design and narrative flow. A vendor writing that down is worth more than a vendor promising the opposite.

And one security line that applies to every deck you build from someone else's template: "Only use Claude for PowerPoint with trusted files." Files from external sources can contain hidden instructions. Testing has identified scenarios where the add-in can be manipulated into extracting sensitive information or taking destructive actions if it is allowed to act without verification. A deck template from a conference organiser is an external file.

Part 2 · covers templates

Why AI decks all look the same, and the mechanical fix

5 min live

There is a real mechanism behind the generic look, and it is not mysterious taste. Microsoft states it plainly: Copilot "tends to default to the first layout in the Slide Master unless explicitly guided". Your brand is sitting in layout seven, and the generator never looked past layout one.

Self-studyThe three-step fix, and why it is mechanical3 min▶
  • Name your layouts clearly. "Title Slide", "Agenda Slide", "Chart Slide". Microsoft's own moderator guidance. A generator cannot choose a layout it cannot name.
  • Save the template as .potx. Not a .pptx you copy each time. The template format is what makes the layouts addressable.
  • Apply the template before generating. Anthropic's best practice for Claude for PowerPoint is worded almost identically: "Start with your template already applied before asking Claude to generate content", and "Verify that outputs match your organization's brand guidelines."

Microsoft also acknowledges that complex layout creation is not fully supported, with no roadmap or timeline given. So the honest position is: simple named layouts applied first will work, and an intricate bespoke master will still need hands on it.

Blank deck, prompt, then re-brand.

Forty slides arrive on the Slide Master's first layout. Applying your template afterwards reflows every text box, and you spend the afternoon nudging them back. Half the charts become pictures of charts.

Open the .potx, then prompt.

The generator places content into named slots that already carry your fonts, colours and spacing. What arrives is a draft in your brand, and the work left is editorial rather than janitorial.

Part 4 · covers curriculum decay

A course is still selling a tool that does not exist

4 min live

This part is short and it is the one you will remember. It is also the reason this course carries a dated source map instead of a confident tone.

The tool. Tome was a genuinely popular AI presentation product with a large user base. It appeared in course catalogues, listicles and recommendations everywhere.

The pivot. Its founders left the presentation product to build Lightfield, an AI-native CRM. Lightfield's own site makes no mention of presentations.

The check. tome.app returns a 404. Not a redirect, not a sunset notice. Gone.

The course. A Coursera course teaching Tome as a presentation platform is still live and still enrollable. Someone will pay for it this week and learn a tool they cannot open.

LiveThe habit this should give you3 min▶

Not cynicism. A thirty-second check, before you spend an evening learning something:

  • Open the vendor's own site. Not a review, not a listicle, not a course description. The product page.
  • Find a dated page. A changelog, a docs page with a last-updated stamp, a pricing page. If the freshest thing you can find is two years old, treat every feature claim as a claim.
  • Treat any AI-tool course older than about a year as history. Not worthless, but not current. This course will need re-verifying too, which is why its source map carries a date on the front.
Real world

Half the "Copilot in Excel requires AutoSave and OneDrive" advice on the internet is now wrong, because Microsoft changed it. The advice was correct when written. Nobody went back.

One honesty note on the Tome story: the widely-quoted shutdown date comes from secondary blogs, so this course does not state it. What it states is what was verified by opening the URLs: the 404, the founders' pivot, and the live course.

Build-along

Build it, in your tool, on your template

★ 22 min · everyone builds

Deck open, template open, outline from session 2 in front of you. This is the session where you stop planning the deck and have one.

Find out which branch you are on. Two minutes, and it changes everything after it. In Microsoft 365, check whether your Copilot label reads Premium, Basic, or Chat Basic, and whether a Designer licence is attached. If a generate button does nothing tonight, this is why.

Open your template first, not a blank deck. If your organisation has a .potx, start from it. If it does not, make one now with three named layouts: Title Slide, Chart Slide, Section Break. Fifteen minutes that you will reuse for years.

Feed it the outline, not the topic. Paste the cut-down outline from session 2, with your sentence headlines already written. You are asking the tool to lay out an argument you already own, which is the only job it does reliably.

Generate, then immediately count the damage. How many slides landed on the wrong layout? How many charts came back as pictures instead of native charts? Write the two numbers down. They tell you whether to keep using this tool for this kind of deck.

Fix the headlines the generator rewrote. Generators love turning a sentence assertion back into a topic label. This is the twenty-one point lever from session 1 and it will quietly get undone. Check every one.

Export early and open it where the room will. Do not leave this to the night before. Export to PPTX now, open it in actual PowerPoint, and look at slide one and your busiest chart. Fonts, spacing, and whether the chart is still a chart.

Run the contrast check on your template's body colour. Below, on your real colours. Note the number. Session 6 turns this into a gate your deck has to pass, and it is the last nine points of the score.

If the tool fights you for more than twenty minutes, stop. You have an outline and written headlines. Building twelve slides by hand from that is roughly an hour, and it is a guaranteed hour. Fighting a generator is not guaranteed anything.

Homework

Before session 6

25 min
  1. Finish the build. All twelve slides, on your template, with your headlines intact. It does not have to be pretty yet. Session 6 is the finishing pass.
  2. Write down your two damage numbers. Slides that landed on the wrong layout, and charts that came back as images. This is your honest record of what your tool actually does.
  3. Do the export test properly. Export, open it in the app the room will use, on a different machine if you can. Note anything that moved.
  4. Record your template's contrast ratio. You will need the number in session 6, and if it is under 4.5:1 you will need a decision from whoever owns the brand.
  5. Optional, and it takes thirty seconds: pick any AI tool a colleague recommended recently and run the three-step currency check on it. Vendor page, dated page, age of the advice.
Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 5 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · You have a Copilot licence, but "add a slide" and "create a presentation about a topic" do nothing. Why?

Microsoft's PowerPoint FAQ lists adding a slide, creating from a topic, creating from a file, organizing, and adding images as Designer-licence features. Without it you get summarization, open-ended Q and A, and basic chat only.

2 · Why do AI-generated decks so often look generic, even on a branded account?

Microsoft states the default-to-layout-one behaviour directly. The fix is mechanical rather than aesthetic: name your layouts, save as .potx, and apply the template before generating.

3 · What is the lesson of the Tome story?

tome.app returns 404 and its founders moved to a different product, yet a course teaching it is still enrollable. Courses and listicles do not get recalled when a product dies. Check the vendor's own page, and check it is dated.
